After dark

The porch after dark

By the end of October it is dark before most people get home, and a lit porch is the one people slow down for. It is also the one you see from the car every evening on the way in, which is really the point of the whole thing. Lighting is what we do all winter, so it is the easiest thing in the world for us to add while we are there.

A porch worth pulling up to

There is a real difference between a few pumpkins by the door and a porch that looks designed, and it comes down to abundance and variety. A pair of genuinely oversize pumpkins low down to anchor each side. A dozen more stacked in behind them at different heights so the whole thing has depth. White, blue and heirloom varieties broken into the orange so the color moves. Minis and gourds spilling down the treads to carry your eye to the door.

That is what we build, and we build it to your house — the width of your steps, the color of your front door, the way the light falls on it in the afternoon. The result is the porch on the street that people slow down for, and the one your family walks past every day for two months.

It is also the backdrop for every photograph anybody takes at your house between now and Thanksgiving, which is worth more than people expect.

Nothing for you to do, start to finish

Booking takes a few minutes and a couple of photos. After that the whole thing is ours: choosing the pumpkins, sourcing the mums in the shade you picked, loading the truck, driving it, carrying it up your steps and arranging it.

You do not need to be home and you do not need to move your car. We text you when we are on the way, the display goes up in about an hour, and it is there when you get back. Nothing gets delivered to your driveway for you to deal with later, and you are never left with a job half done.

Then at the end of the season we come and take all of it away, on a day you choose. That part is already in the price, so there is nothing to arrange, nothing to haul out to the curb and nothing to find room for in the garage.

Real pumpkins, mums, bales and cornstalks

Everything comes from growers, and the mix is what makes a display look considered. Oversize field pumpkins give it weight at the bottom. Large and medium jack varieties build the height. White ghost pumpkins, blue Jarrahdale, green and warty heirlooms give the color somewhere to go, and minis and ornamental gourds fill in and run down the steps.

Mums are the color you see from the road, so they go where the display meets the walkway. We buy large, hardy garden mums in full bud, which is what keeps them coming into flower right through the season. Rust, burgundy, gold, cream and purple all look wonderful — pick a favorite, or leave it to us.

Straw bales give the display depth, lifting the back of each pile so it has shape rather than sitting flat against the house. And cornstalks give it height: tied to a column, a porch post or a lamp post, they are what makes a display scale up to a tall front and finish the whole thing off.

Up any week in October, and it runs to Thanksgiving

Displays go up from the last week of September right through to the end of October, so you can have it whenever suits you — in time for the first pumpkin patch weekend, in time for the school photographs, or in time for trick-or-treat. Book early and you get the pick of the dates; we only take 30 porches, so the calendar closes when it closes.

Thanksgiving falls on November 26 this year, which is as late as it ever gets. That makes for a long, generous season: even a display that goes up on the last day of October has a full four weeks to stand, and one that goes up at the start of the month is there for the best part of two.

Pick the week that suits you when you book, and we will confirm the day. If you have people coming for a particular weekend, tell us and we will have it up in good time.

Takedown works the same way: you say when, and we confirm the day rather than just turning up. Some people like the porch clear the morning after Halloween. Plenty keep it right through November, because a porch full of pumpkins and cornstalks still looks like fall long after the costumes are put away. The The week after Thanksgiving is the latest we collect — the start of December this year — which is usually exactly when you want the porch ready for Christmas.
