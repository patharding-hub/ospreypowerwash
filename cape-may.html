PRESSURE WASHINGIN CAPE MAY

Cape May has the most delicate housing stock in South Jersey. Painted Victorian woodwork, gingerbread trim and a historic district that people rightly care about. None of it should ever meet a pressure washer.

A Victorian is soft wash only

Painted wood, decorative trim and old glazing putty do not survive pressure. What they need is low pressure and chemistry: we kill the growth and rinse it off, the paint stays where it is, and the detail that makes the house worth looking at is still there afterwards.

If anyone offers to pressure wash a painted Victorian, that is the moment to stop the conversation. The damage is not subtle and it is not cheap to put right.

Damp, salt and shade together

Being nearly surrounded by water, Cape May holds damp longer than anywhere inland, and the older houses have the shaded porches and north walls to go with it. Green film on painted clapboard is the standard job here, and it comes off cleanly.
